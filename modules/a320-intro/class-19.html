CLASS 19 · ATA 52 / 56

Doors are part of the pressure vessel — and the evacuation system.

En operación diaria, el riesgo dominante suele ser procedural: una puerta puede estar físicamente cerrada y aun así no estar en la configuración operacional correcta. Esta clase conecta geometría, presión diferencial, locking, slide arming, cargo-door hydraulics, cockpit security y ventanas.

02 · PRESSURE-SEAL LOGIC LAB

Pressure helps keep the door in the airplane.

Passenger/crew doors are plug-type structures: pressure differential loads the door against its frame. Ground safety is about ensuring the pressure vessel is actually equalized before operation.

Not a strength contest. In flight, normal opening of a passenger/crew plug-type door is prevented by geometry + differential pressure, not merely by an electronic lock.

03 · ARMED / DISARMED LAB

Slide state changes what “open” means.

Select slide state and opening direction. This separates three ideas that are often mixed together: door locking, slide arming and the way the door is opened.

A320 design point: opening a passenger/crew door from outside automatically disarms the slide mechanism. That design feature reduces inadvertent deployment, but operator procedures still govern ground opening.

04 · TURNAROUND DISCIPLINE

Arm for flight. Disarm for normal ground opening.

This timeline is a procedural model, not an operator-specific command sequence. Exact terminology and cross-check flow belong to the airline SOP.

ERROR 1 · ARMED AT THE GATE

Normal inside opening may deploy a slide. Personnel injury and aircraft/ground-equipment damage are possible, followed by major operational disruption.

ERROR 2 · DISARMED FOR EVACUATION

The selected exit may open without providing its normal escape-slide function. This is why slide state is a crew resource-management problem, not a cosmetic indication.

06 · CARGO DOOR LOGIC

Ground hydraulics with deliberate isolation.

In the classic A320 architecture, cargo doors use yellow hydraulic power. During powered cargo-door operation, the system isolates PTU interaction so the operation does not drive unintended transfer behavior.

Ground-only mindset: cargo-door hydraulics are a turnaround/maintenance interface. A cargo door not confirmed closed and locked is a pressure-boundary problem before departure.

07 · COCKPIT DOOR

Security partition — with decompression protection.

The reinforced cockpit door has a security role. Decompression panels protect the partition against a sudden pressure differential between flight deck and cabin. Exact access-code and override procedures are operator-security material.

08 · WINDOWS · ATA 56

Visibility, structure and escape.

Windows are not decorative openings. They are engineered parts of the pressure vessel and, in the cockpit, part of the emergency-escape architecture.

Passenger-window nuance: the window assembly has multiple panes. The small engineered breather/bleed feature manages the inter-pane cavity and condensation; avoid teaching an oversimplified universal statement about exactly which single pane always carries all differential pressure.

10 · PILOT INTELLIGENCE

State awareness before movement.

The system lesson is simple: before operating an opening, know its pressure state, lock state, evacuation configuration and operational context.

PRESSURE

Is the pressure vessel equalized, or could residual differential pressure still matter?

LOCKING

Closed is not enough. Is the door/opening confirmed in the required secured/locked state?

EVACUATION

For passenger/crew doors, is the slide state correct for flight, evacuation or normal ground opening?

CONTEXT

Inside vs outside opening, ground service, emergency evacuation and cargo handling are different operational contexts.
